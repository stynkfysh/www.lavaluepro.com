Wilmington home and condo appraisals for estates, trusts, and taxes

Historic harbor town of older bungalows amid refineries and port terminals

Phineas Banning developed Wilmington as the original Los Angeles harbor town in the late 1850s; the City of Los Angeles annexed it in 1909, and port terminals, refineries and the Wilmington oil field now surround it. Assignments usually involve modest older houses on small lots, and industrial proximity, added units and permit history are the recurring questions.

Homes in Wilmington

Older blocks near Avalon Boulevard and Banning Park include early twentieth-century bungalows and cottages. One-story postwar houses cover much of the rest, on modest lots that sometimes carry a second dwelling or later additions. Apartment buildings mix with single-family streets, and the housing changes noticeably where neighborhoods meet industrial land.

What moves value in Wilmington

Distance from refinery fence lines, tank farms, rail yards and truck routes shapes buyer response, so streets near Banning Park and the waterfront park sell on different terms than homes beside industrial land. Port employment and quick access to the 110 and Pacific Coast Highway keep local demand steady.

Appraisal considerations in Wilmington

Industrial and oil-field adjacency, including active drill sites, refinery operations and port truck routes, is documented for the effective date and gauged against sales with comparable exposure. Rear units and additions are checked against Los Angeles Department of Building and Safety records before counting as living area, and because many older houses have seen limited updating, condition ratings lean heavily on client photos.

For a past date of death, how does refinery proximity affect a Wilmington home's value?

We measure it with sales rather than assume it. Comparables are chosen at similar distances from refinery fence lines, truck routes and drill sites, and any difference the paired sales show as of the effective date is applied as a location adjustment and explained in the report.
