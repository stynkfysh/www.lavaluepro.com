Homes in Beverly Hills

The Flats, between Santa Monica and Sunset boulevards, is a planned grid of wide, tree-lined streets with large lots holding period-revival and newer custom homes. North of Sunset the terrain turns to hillside, including Trousdale Estates, developed in the 1950s and 1960s on land that was once part of the Doheny estate and known for mid-century modern houses with city views. South of Wilshire the lots are smaller, and single-family streets sit beside the city's heaviest concentration of multifamily buildings, including condominiums.

What moves value in Beverly Hills

Land value drives much of the market, since many buyers acquire older houses in the Flats and the hills to remodel or replace. Views, privacy and lot frontage separate hillside sales, and buyers often weigh city services and the municipal address against nearby Los Angeles neighborhoods that share the 90210 mailing name. Condominium values south of Wilshire depend on building age, amenities and association costs.

Appraisal considerations in Beverly Hills

The city designates the area north of Sunset Boulevard a Very High Fire Hazard Severity Zone, which brings brush-clearance duties and can affect insurability. Trousdale Estates has its own view-protection ordinance, and the city runs a permanent Mills Act program for designated landmarks. Comparable selection must also separate true Beverly Hills properties from Beverly Hills Post Office homes, which carry a 90210 address but lie in the City of Los Angeles.

Does a Beverly Hills Post Office address compare directly with homes inside Beverly Hills city limits?

Not automatically. Beverly Hills Post Office homes sit in the City of Los Angeles, with different zoning, permitting and city services, so we treat them as a distinct market and separate or adjust comparables when the sales evidence shows a difference.
