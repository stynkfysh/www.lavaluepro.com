Westside & Malibu

From the bluffs of Santa Monica to the canyons of Malibu and the flats of Mar Vista, the Westside packs one of the county's widest value ranges into a few miles. Distance to the coast, hillside views, school boundaries, and condo versus single-family product all move the numbers, and the January 2025 Palisades Fire created a pre-fire and post-fire dividing line for retrospective work in parts of the area.

South LA & Baldwin Hills

Baldwin Hills, Leimert Park, West Adams, View Park–Windsor Hills, and Ladera Heights hold some of Los Angeles' most architecturally distinctive single-family neighborhoods, from Victorian and Craftsman houses in West Adams to mid-century homes on the Baldwin Hills slopes. Many of these homes have stayed in one family for decades, which makes the stepped-up basis especially consequential when they pass to the next generation.

Hollywood, Central & Eastside LA

Hollywood, the Eastside, and the urban core mix 1920s hillside homes, Spanish and Craftsman bungalows, historic districts with design review, and a deep condo and loft market downtown and in Koreatown. Values here turn on block-level detail: stairs and street access on hillside lots, preservation-overlay rules, views, and how well a condominium association is run.

Burbank, Glendale & Crescenta Valley

Burbank, Glendale, La Cañada Flintridge, and La Crescenta–Montrose sit between the Verdugo Mountains and the San Gabriels, where level tracts give way quickly to sloped foothill lots. Studio-district demand, Glendale's large condominium inventory, and fire-zone and view questions on the upper streets all shape value.

San Fernando Valley

The San Fernando Valley's post-war ranch tracts, hillside streets south of Ventura Boulevard, and condo corridors along the major boulevards make it one of the county's deepest pools of comparable sales. That depth suits desktop analysis, although lot size, pools, accessory dwelling units, and freeway or airport influence still separate one street from the next.

Santa Clarita & Las Virgenes

Santa Clarita, Stevenson Ranch, and the Las Virgenes cities of Calabasas, Agoura Hills, and Westlake Village are dominated by planned communities built from the 1960s onward. Homeowners associations, Mello-Roos assessments, gated versus open tracts, and matching floor plans within a builder's tract are recurring factors in these reports.

Antelope Valley

Lancaster and Palmdale are the Antelope Valley's established cities, with tract neighborhoods ranging from mid-century homes near the original town centers to 2000s subdivisions on the west and east sides. Our coverage here is the subdivided city neighborhoods; the valley's rural acreage is outside the scope of a desktop report.

  • LancasterCity
  • PalmdaleCity

Pasadena & West San Gabriel Valley

Pasadena and the western San Gabriel Valley combine Craftsman and period-revival neighborhoods, large-lot cities such as San Marino and Arcadia, and steadily traded suburbs from Alhambra to El Monte. Historic designations, lot size and rebuild potential, and, for Altadena and nearby foothill streets, the January 2025 Eaton Fire are central valuation issues.

East San Gabriel & Pomona Valleys

From Azusa and Glendora at the base of the San Gabriels to Diamond Bar and Rowland Heights in the Puente Hills, the eastern valleys mix college-town neighborhoods, 1950s to 1970s tracts, and hillside view communities. Freeway access, foothill fire zones, and gated or association-managed tracts are the variables we watch most closely.

Gateway Cities

The Gateway Cities southeast of downtown are built largely on modest post-war lots with a steady local buyer base, which produces reliable comparable sales for desktop work. Rear units and second dwellings, industrial and freeway adjacency, and the permit history of additions are the details that most often move value.

South Bay & Palos Verdes

The South Bay runs from the walk streets of the beach cities through Torrance's mid-century tracts to the view homes of the Palos Verdes Peninsula. Distance to the sand, ocean views, LAX flight paths, and, on parts of the Peninsula, active landslide areas create wide value differences over short distances.

Long Beach, Harbor & Catalina

Long Beach and the harbor communities pair historic neighborhoods such as Belmont Shore and Bixby Knolls with port-side San Pedro and Wilmington, oil-era Signal Hill, and Avalon's island-only market on Catalina. Historic districts, water proximity, and condominium associations are common factors, and Avalon's limited sales call for especially careful comparable selection.

Outside our coverage. Desktop reports depend on a steady record of comparable sales, so our coverage stops at the county's predominantly rural areas, including Acton, Agua Dulce, Leona Valley, Lake Hughes, Littlerock, Pearblossom, Lake Los Angeles and the rest of the unincorporated Antelope Valley, the Santa Monica Mountains canyons such as Topanga, and the equestrian estate cities of Hidden Hills, Rolling Hills, Bradbury, and La Habra Heights. Largely industrial cities (Vernon, Industry, Irwindale) are not covered either.
