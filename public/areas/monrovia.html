Homes in Monrovia

The blocks around Old Town retain Victorian, Craftsman, and period revival houses, many with detached garages. Postwar ranch homes fill much of the rest of the city, while larger custom and view homes sit along the northern foothill streets. South Monrovia adds smaller tract homes, apartments, and townhomes, and the Station Square district around the A Line station brings newer attached housing.

What moves value in Monrovia

Old Town's walkable district and the A Line draw buyers who value historic character and transit, while foothill streets attract buyers seeking views and space. Historic houses with intact detail compete differently from remodeled tract homes, and proximity to the 210 Freeway or the technology and industrial uses in the south of the city can shape individual values.

Appraisal considerations in Monrovia

Some older Monrovia houses may be locally designated or carry Mills Act contracts, so we confirm historic status through city and county records. Foothill parcels need attention to slope, defensible space, and fire-hazard mapping, and deep lots in the older core may support an ADU or lot split, though parcel-level constraints decide whether that potential is real.

Is a restored Victorian in Monrovia compared only with other Victorians?

Not necessarily, because few sell in any year. We start with the closest historic-home sales in Old Town and nearby districts, then widen the search to similar-era houses in comparable foothill cities, adjusting for size, condition, and lot, and explaining why each sale was chosen.
