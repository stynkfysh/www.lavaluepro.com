Hollywood home and condo appraisals for estates, trusts, and taxes

Historic bungalow tracts, older apartments, and condos below the Hollywood Hills

Hollywood incorporated in 1903 and consolidated with Los Angeles in 1910, and today it runs roughly from Franklin Avenue and Hollywood Boulevard south to Melrose Avenue, between Western Avenue and La Brea Avenue or the West Hollywood line. Beyond its entertainment landmarks, it holds small tracts of 1910s and 1920s houses and a large condominium stock, so families and attorneys bring us historic-district bungalows, inherited condos, and past-date values for trust funding.

Homes in Hollywood

Single-family streets cluster in a few historic tracts: Spaulding Square, built between 1916 and 1921 with mostly Colonial-style bungalows; Sunset Square, with Craftsman, Spanish Colonial Revival, and American Colonial Revival houses; and Hollywood Grove, a foothill enclave of Craftsman and Colonial Revival bungalows. Above Franklin Avenue, Whitley Heights was designed between 1918 and 1928 as a Mediterranean-style hillside village. Elsewhere, apartment buildings from many decades fill most blocks, joined by newer low- and mid-rise projects, and condominiums are a common form of ownership housing.

What moves value in Hollywood

Buyers weigh walkable access to Hollywood Boulevard, the studios, and the Hollywood/Vine and Hollywood/Highland Metro stations against traffic, tourist activity, and noise near the 101. Intact houses in the historic tracts trade in a separate submarket from condominiums, whose values follow building age, parking, association costs, and amenities. Studio and media employment close by adds to demand.

Appraisal considerations in Hollywood

Hollywood's Historic Preservation Overlay Zones include Whitley Heights, Spaulding Square, Hollywood Grove, and Sunset Square, where exterior changes require review, and Whitley Heights is also listed on the National Register of Historic Places. The Hollywood Freeway split Whitley Heights when it was built, and homes close to the 101 are measured against sales in similar freeway-adjacent locations. Condominiums are matched within the same building or with buildings of similar age and amenities.

Does the Whitley Heights historic zone limit what a buyer could do with Mom's house?

Yes. Exterior alterations, additions, and demolition in an HPOZ require preservation review, and buyers account for that when they bid. We therefore rely on sales within Whitley Heights or similar hillside historic districts, so the market itself reflects the restriction rather than a flat adjustment.
