West Hollywood home and condo appraisals for estates, trusts, and taxes

Compact city of period apartment buildings, condominiums and small-lot houses

West Hollywood, incorporated in 1984, occupies a compact strip between Beverly Hills and Hollywood, with the Sunset Strip along its northern edge. Most of its housing is multifamily, so the questions we see usually involve condominium values for estates and trusts, date-of-separation values for a townhome, or the value of a single-family house in West Hollywood West or the Norma Triangle.

Homes in West Hollywood

Apartment and condominium buildings dominate, including Spanish Revival and Art Deco apartment buildings from the 1920s concentrated around Fountain Avenue, Harper Avenue and Havenhurst Drive. Single-family houses are concentrated in West Hollywood West and the Norma Triangle, where many homes have been expanded or rebuilt. Condominiums range from converted older buildings to newer mid-rise projects, so building age can change sharply within a few blocks.

What moves value in West Hollywood

Location relative to the Sunset Strip, Santa Monica Boulevard and the Melrose and Robertson design district shapes demand, and walkability is a large part of what buyers pay for. The single-family supply is small, so houses here tend to draw buyers who are also shopping Beverly Hills and the Hollywood Hills. Condominium values turn on building quality, parking, amenities and association finances.

Appraisal considerations in West Hollywood

The city's Rent Stabilization Ordinance covers much of the rental stock, which matters when a condominium is tenant-occupied on the effective date or when sales from converted buildings serve as comparables. Many period apartment buildings carry historic interest, so designation status should be checked for any property with redevelopment potential. Special assessments and reserve levels vary widely among older associations and belong in the condominium analysis.

How do you value a tenant-occupied West Hollywood condominium in an estate?

We start with sales of comparable units in similar buildings, then consider whether a tenancy under the city's rent rules would narrow the buyer pool or reduce the price as of the effective date. Where the evidence shows occupied units selling differently, that difference is reflected in the opinion of value.
