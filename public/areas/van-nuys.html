Valley civic center, airport neighborhoods and 1940s–1950s bungalow streets

Van Nuys, founded in 1911 and annexed to Los Angeles in 1915, serves as the San Fernando Valley's civic center, anchored by the 1932 Valley Municipal Building and surrounded by blocks of older single-family homes. Trustees, executors and family-law attorneys bring assignments ranging from 1940s bungalows to condominiums, frequently with garage conversions or rear units that have to be sorted out first.

Homes in Van Nuys

Single-family building began with the 1911 townsite, and the Van Nuys Historic Preservation Overlay Zone, adopted in 2005 as the Valley's first HPOZ, protects Craftsman, Colonial Revival, Spanish Colonial Revival and English Revival houses alongside later Minimal Traditional and ranch designs. Much of the remaining single-family stock consists of 1940s and 1950s bungalows, cottages and ranch houses on modest lots, many expanded over the years. Apartments account for a large share of the housing, and condominium and townhome complexes give buyers an attached choice.

What moves value in Van Nuys

Buyers are drawn by a central location with access to the 405 and 101, Metro G Line stops and Metrolink service, and many compare Van Nuys directly with neighboring Sherman Oaks and Lake Balboa. Condition, permitted living area and street setting explain most differences between sales, with homes near industrial corridors, the airport or heavy traffic drawing a narrower pool.

Appraisal considerations in Van Nuys

Van Nuys Airport, with two parallel runways, ranks among the busiest general aviation airports in the world, so homes beneath arrival and departure paths should be compared with sales in a similar noise setting. Inside the HPOZ, exterior alterations require review, and garage conversions and rear units elsewhere should be checked against permit records before any value is assigned to them.

Does Van Nuys Airport noise lower the value of a house near the runways?

It can, depending on how directly the home sits under arrival and departure paths. We test for an influence by comparing sales close to the airport with similar homes farther away, and we make an adjustment only when the data show buyers paying less for that location.
