Homes in Maywood

The land was a ranch until 1919, when May Wood lent her name to its subdivision into home tracts, and the residential grid filled in during the following decades. Single-family homes sit on compact lots, reflecting the city's dense street grid. Rear houses, added units, and small apartment buildings appear throughout the city, and renters occupy a large majority of homes.

What moves value in Maywood

Maywood's density means owner-occupied single-family sales are limited, so we often draw comparables from Bell, Huntington Park, and Commerce. Close-in access to downtown Los Angeles and to the Long Beach Freeway via Atlantic Boulevard supports demand, while industrial edges near the river temper it on some blocks.

Appraisal considerations in Maywood

Where a parcel holds more than one dwelling, unit count and permit history are often decisive, and we report undocumented units separately. On such compact lots, any enlarged house deserves verification of its living area against the assessor's records. Blocks beside industrial uses on the city's edges may call for location adjustments.

Will a buyer pay more for a Maywood lot with two houses than for one house?

Generally yes when both units are legal and in reasonable condition, because the property offers living space plus rental income. We verify permits, compare against other multi-dwelling sales, and adjust for unit size and condition. Undocumented units are discussed separately so the conclusion reflects real market risk.
