Homes in Playa del Rey

Lower Playa del Rey occupies low-lying ground that was once wetlands and dunes, with houses, apartments and condominiums near Culver Boulevard and Del Rey Lagoon, including the closely built 1956 apartment cluster locals call the Jungle. Above it, the Del Rey Hills, also called the Bluffs, rise along the coast with custom homes positioned for ocean views. The eastern part of the community is the most densely built, with apartment and condominium complexes. The original 1921 subdivision was Palisades del Rey, and its southern section, Surfridge, was cleared for LAX between 1966 and 1975.

What moves value in Playa del Rey

Ocean views, distance to the beach and elevation drive the largest value differences, with bluff-top homes trading in a different tier from lower streets. Buyers weigh beach access and the small-town commercial strip against airport noise and limited road connections. Condominiums appeal to buyers working in Playa Vista, Westchester and the South Bay.

Appraisal considerations in Playa del Rey

LAX lies directly south, so aircraft noise and flight-path position should be measured with paired sales rather than assumed. The community is in the coastal zone, and low-lying parcels near the lagoon and creek should be checked for flood-zone status. View premiums on the bluffs depend on how protected and how wide the view is, and condominium comparables should come from complexes with similar age, amenities and association costs.

How much does an ocean view add to a Playa del Rey bluff home?

There is no fixed amount; we measure it from local sales by comparing view and non-view homes that are otherwise similar, close to the effective date. The width and permanence of the view and the home's elevation all influence the adjustment.
