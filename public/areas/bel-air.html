Gated estate lots and winding hillside streets climbing toward Mulholland Drive

Bel Air is a hillside community in the City of Los Angeles north of Sunset Boulevard, founded in 1923 by Alphonzo Bell and planned around large residential lots in the Santa Monica Mountains. Valuation requests here usually involve estate properties held in trust, date-of-death values for heirs settling a family residence, and divorce valuations in which land value and improvements must be analyzed separately.

Homes in Bel Air

The original Bel Air Estates subdivision and the streets inside the East Gate hold the oldest and often largest estates. Beyond the gates, the community extends north along canyon roads such as Stone Canyon Road and Roscomare Road, with lots ranging from level pads to steep slopes. Houses from the community's early decades stand beside mid-century homes and recent large-scale rebuilds. Detached single-family homes dominate the housing stock.

What moves value in Bel Air

Land value, privacy, usable flat area and views carry much of the weight, along with a location inside the East or West Gate and the quality of road access. The buyer pool for estates is national and international, and sales are infrequent, so the market for any one property can be thin. Smaller homes in the upper canyons tend to draw a different, more local set of buyers.

Appraisal considerations in Bel Air

Bel Air's hillsides fall within a Very High Fire Hazard Severity Zone, and the community lost hundreds of homes in the 1961 fire and several more in a 2017 brush fire, so brush clearance, fire access and insurance terms deserve attention. Hillside lots are governed by the City's Baseline Hillside Ordinance, which limits floor area and grading, so the size of home a lot could support should be confirmed before land value is concluded. On large estates, adjustments for lot area and amenities should be supported by sales of comparable scale rather than by smaller homes nearby.

How do you value a Bel Air estate when few comparable sales closed that year?

We widen the search in time and geography as needed, drawing on sales from Holmby Hills, Beverly Hills and Brentwood when Bel Air itself is thin, and adjust those sales for market change to the effective date. Land sales and the replacement cost of the improvements serve as cross-checks when the sales evidence is limited.
