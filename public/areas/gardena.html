Homes in Gardena

Gardena began as an 1887 subdivision and spent its early decades in berry fields and truck farms, absorbing the neighboring Moneta settlement when the city incorporated in 1930. Most housing arrived after World War II as tracts of one-story stucco houses on standard lots, followed by apartments and condominium complexes in later decades. The ground is flat throughout, and Hollypark and Strawberry Park remain common neighborhood references.

What moves value in Gardena

A central location and freeway access give Gardena a broad commuter buyer pool, and detached houses are usually measured against nearby Torrance, Hawthorne and Harbor Gateway sales. Condominium and townhome comparisons turn heavily on HOA dues, project age and amenities, and blocks bordering industrial districts or busy arterials are compared separately from quiet residential streets.

Appraisal considerations in Gardena

Gardena ZIP codes also serve parts of Harbor Gateway in the City of Los Angeles and several unincorporated county pockets, so the governing jurisdiction is established before any permit research. Condominium files call for dues, special assessments and any pending project litigation at the date of value, and additions in the postwar tracts are reconciled with permits and assessor square footage.

Mom's house has a Gardena address but sits in Los Angeles; which sales apply?

Sales from both sides of the line can qualify. Harbor Gateway blocks sharing Gardena ZIP codes usually trade much like their Gardena neighbors, so we choose comparables by location, age and size while checking permits and rules with the City of Los Angeles, which governs the parcel.
