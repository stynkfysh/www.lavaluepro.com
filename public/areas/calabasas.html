Homes in Calabasas

Much of the city consists of planned communities built from the 1960s through the 2000s, including Calabasas Park, Mountain View Estates, Calabasas Hills, and gated enclaves such as The Oaks and Calabasas Park Estates. Park Moderne, also called the Bird Streets, and the Calabasas Highlands hold older, more individual homes on hillside lots. Townhomes and condominiums, many in association-run complexes, form a sizable attached segment.

What moves value in Calabasas

Privacy, gated access, and views drive the upper tier, while condominiums and townhomes offer a lower entry point. Ventura Freeway access and the surrounding mountain open space shape demand, and values vary sharply between communities, so the specific tract and its association rules matter.

Appraisal considerations in Calabasas

Hillside and canyon parcels sit in wildfire-prone terrain, and the 2018 Woolsey Fire reached the city, damaging Grape Arbor Park, so we weigh brush-clearance duties, insurance availability, and any fire history of the parcel. Gated communities often carry master and sub-association dues, and older canyon homes may involve access easements or nonconforming improvements that need careful records review.

How are guard-gated Calabasas homes compared with non-gated sales?

We prefer sales from the same gated community, because privacy, amenities, and association costs are already reflected in their prices. When none are recent enough, we use similar gated enclaves nearby and explain any adjustment for differences in security, amenities, and dues.
