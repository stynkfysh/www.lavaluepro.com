Why heirs and trustees order a date-specific value

An inherited home's tax basis generally becomes its fair market value on the date of death. That one number decides how much gain is taxed when the home is sold, how trust shares are balanced, and whether a family buyout is fair, so it deserves more than an online estimate.

  • Date-of-Death Appraisals

    The fair market value of a home on the day its owner died, for basis records, trust accounting, and estate files.

  • Step-Up in Basis Appraisals

    Documents the new tax basis of an inherited home so capital gains or rental depreciation are calculated correctly.

  • Trust Administration Appraisals

    A neutral value for successor trustees who must account to beneficiaries, distribute property, or arrange a buyout.

  • Estate Settlement & Estate Tax Appraisals

    Values for executors and administrators settling an estate, filing Form 706, or deciding whether to sell or distribute a home.

  • Gift Tax Appraisals

    Fair market value on the date a home or an interest in one is given, prepared for Form 709 reporting.

  • Prop 19 Parent-Child Transfer Appraisals

    Checks the market value that drives the Prop 19 property tax calculation when a parent's home passes to a child.

  • Divorce & Date-of-Separation Appraisals

    A neutral market value of the family home, current or as of the date of separation, for settlement and buyouts.

  • Family Sale & Co-Owner Buyout Appraisals

    Fair market value for sales between relatives, sibling buyouts of inherited homes, and dividing co-owned property.

  • Retrospective Appraisals

    Value as of any past date: a gift, a transfer into a trust, a date of separation, or the start of a tax year.

What a desktop appraisal is, and why it fits estate work

A desktop appraisal is a complete appraisal analysis without a site visit. We draw on county records, multiple listing service history, and the photos you send, then analyze comparable sales from the right period and reach a supported opinion of value under USPAP. No one has to unlock a vacant house or coordinate access with family members.

How a request becomes a signed report

Four steps, all by email and online forms.

  1. Send the request

    Use the order form to give us the address, the purpose, and the date the value is needed for. It takes about three minutes and commits you to nothing.

  2. We confirm and invoice

    Within one business day we confirm the property qualifies for a desktop report, confirm the fee, and email a secure online invoice.

  3. Pay and share photos

    Once the invoice is paid, the assignment starts. We ask for photos of the home, and for past dates, anything that shows its condition then.

  4. Receive the signed report

    The finished appraisal arrives as a PDF, typically 5–7 business days after payment, ready for your CPA, attorney, or file.

Common questions

Will the IRS accept a desktop appraisal?

Federal tax rules call for a well-supported fair market value; they do not require that the appraiser inspect the interior. Certified desktop reports are widely used to document date-of-death values and stepped-up basis. Your CPA decides what support your particular return needs.

Which properties do you appraise?

Single-family homes and condominiums, including townhome-style condos, in our Los Angeles County coverage area. We do not take 2–4 unit buildings, vacant land, commercial or mixed-use property, or homes under construction. If you are unsure, send the request and we will tell you.

What is the difference between Basic and Standard?

Both use the same research and comparable sales analysis and reach a fully supported value. The Standard report adds a location map, comparable sale photos, a market conditions analysis, and a fuller written explanation, which CPAs and trusts with several beneficiaries usually prefer.

Can you appraise a home as of a date in the past?

Yes. Retrospective values as of a date of death, a gift, a transfer, or a date of separation are routine, and they cost the same as a current-value report.
