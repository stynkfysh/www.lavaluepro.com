Divorce and date-of-separation appraisals in Los Angeles County

Dividing a Los Angeles County home in a divorce starts with a value both sides can accept. A desktop appraisal gives spouses, their attorneys, or a mediator a neutral, documented market value as of today, as of the date of separation, or both.

Common dates
Current value; date of separation; both when the parties disagree on which applies
Typical users
Spouses, family law attorneys, mediators
Properties
Single-family homes and condominiums
Fees
$299 Basic Desktop or $449 Standard Desktop
Delivery
3+ business days

Current value or date of separation

California Family Code §2552 generally values community assets as near as practicable to the time of trial, but the court can use another date for good cause, and settlement talks frequently reference the date of separation. When the right date is unsettled, a report with both values lets everyone see the difference before negotiating.

Built for a buyout

When one spouse keeps the home, the buyout figure depends on the market value less what is owed against the property. A neutral appraisal removes the most argued-over number from the conversation.

Neutral by design

We do not advocate for either side. When both spouses agree to share one appraiser, the report can name both as intended users, which often saves the cost and delay of dueling valuations.

When a desktop report fits

Desktop appraisals work well for mediation, settlement negotiations, and agreed buyouts. If the value is likely to be litigated, or the home has significant condition issues, tell us before ordering so we can discuss whether an inspection-based assignment is a better choice.

Questions about divorce & date-of-separation appraisals

Can both spouses use the same appraisal?

Yes. A jointly ordered report can name both parties and their attorneys as intended users.

Do you need access to the house?

No. The analysis uses public records, sales data, and photos the parties provide. Either spouse can supply photos; it helps when both agree on them.

Can you value the home as of a past date of separation?

Yes. Retrospective values are routine in family law matters.
