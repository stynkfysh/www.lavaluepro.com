Trust administration appraisals for Los Angeles County trustees

Successor trustees use an independent appraisal to value trust real estate at the settlor's death, divide property fairly among beneficiaries, document distributions, and support a buyout when one beneficiary keeps the house. A certified desktop report gives the trustee a defensible number without scheduling an inspection of a home that may be vacant or occupied by family.

Typical effective date
The settlor's date of death, or a current date for a distribution or sale
Typical users
Successor trustees, beneficiaries, trust attorneys, and CPAs
Properties
Single-family homes and condominiums held in the trust
Fees
$299 Basic Desktop or $449 Standard Desktop per property
Delivery
3+ business days

A neutral number for a fiduciary

A trustee owes beneficiaries impartial treatment and accurate accountings. Relying on a broker's opinion or an online estimate invites second-guessing, especially when one beneficiary is buying out the others. An appraisal by a state-certified appraiser who has no stake in the outcome gives the trustee documentation that is easy to explain.

Equal shares, buyouts, and in-kind distributions

When one sibling keeps the family home and the others receive cash or other assets, the home's value sets the size of every share. The same is true when properties are distributed in kind. We can value the property as of the date of death for tax purposes and, if the distribution happens much later, as of a current date as well.

Several properties, one request

Trusts in Los Angeles County often hold more than one property, such as a family home and a condo, or a house in the Valley and another in the South Bay. List each address in a single request and we will quote them together.

Working alongside the trust's advisers

Reports are addressed to the client who orders them and can name the trust's attorney and CPA as intended users so the same report serves accounting, tax basis, and distribution decisions.

Questions about trust administration appraisals

Who usually orders the appraisal, the trustee or a beneficiary?

Most often the trustee, as part of administering the trust. A beneficiary can also order one; tell us who will rely on the report so it names the right intended users.

Can one report serve both tax and distribution purposes?

Frequently, yes, when both rely on the date-of-death value. If the distribution occurs well after the death, a second effective date may be needed; we will flag that when we review your request.

What if the beneficiaries disagree about value?

A neutral report is often the starting point that resolves the disagreement. If you expect the value to be contested in court, ask us before ordering whether a desktop report is the right fit.
